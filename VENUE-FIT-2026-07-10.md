## 1. Is the completed evidence ENOUGH for TASLP?

TASLP judges a paper on technical soundness, empirical rigor, reproducibility, and relevance to
the speech/audio/language community. Unlike TMLR it has no formal "no-novelty-bar" clause, but it
routinely publishes systems/benchmark/audit papers whose contribution is a rigorously validated
capability rather than a new theorem — a certified-triage *system* with a distribution-free
guarantee, a debunking audit, and a released tool sits squarely in scope. Crucially, TASLP does
reject papers that misrepresent their relationship to prior speech work, so the Ernez et al.
delta paragraph is, if anything, *more* load-bearing here than at TMLR. Against that bar:

**The two headline claims are each supported by an independent, verdict-symmetric body of
evidence.**

- **C1 — certified selective triage (the guarantee).** On Aishell-1 official test (Paraformer,
  touched once), the LTT selective-risk certificate holds at α=2%, δ=0.1 with **0/20
  violations** across 20 speaker-level calibration reseeds, at **85.7–96.2% acceptance** and
  **accepted-set macro-CER 1.00–1.53%** — comfortably under target. The certificate degrades
  gracefully under distribution shift: with the *clean-calibrated* gate applied to ESC-50
  additive noise, violations are **0/20 at 25 dB and 15 dB** and **1/20 at 5 dB** (still within
  the δ=0.1 tolerance, 0.05 ≤ 0.1), with accepted-set CER 1.22%/1.32%/1.62%. And it fails
  **honestly and informatively** where it must: the Whisper zero-shot Mandarin backbone
  (Aishell macro-CER 28.1%, THCHS-30 15.8%) is **VACUOUS-AT-TARGET at every α ∈ {1,2,3,5}%**
  (0% acceptance) — no low-CER accepted region exists to certify. This is exactly the design's
  K4 vacuity outcome, and it is publishable: the certificate's usefulness is contingent on the
  backbone being strong enough, and the paper states the contingency rather than hiding it.

- **C2 — the debunk-or-confirm audit.** The realized Holm family is **m = 12** — the two
  auditable scores {s1 length-normalized log-posterior, s2 weakest-link} on the six
  (backbone × condition) cells that produced ≥99% non-null scores: Paraformer on {clean, 5, 15,
  25 dB} and Whisper on {Aishell clean, THCHS-30}. **All 12 reject the analytic
  random-deferral null** (matched-abstention permutation, n_perm=2000) at
  **p_Holm = 0.006** (the permutation floor). Field-standard ASR confidence carries real
  selective skill — across two architectures, three noise levels, and two corpora, and
  crucially **even on the weak Whisper backbone where the certificate is vacuous**. The clean
  separation of "audit fires / certificate cannot" is itself a contribution.

**Novelty positioning is defensible and load-bearing.** The 2026-07-10 scoop scan is clean: no
found work does certified accept/defer triage with a distribution-free accepted-set WER/CER
bound for ASR. The must-cite anchor (Ernez et al. 2023, PMLR 204:16–35, CRC-controlled WER over
sentence prediction *sets*) is positioned with an explicit delta paragraph (set-valued output
vs population-level accepted-set risk under an accept/defer decision). The two post-freeze 2026
papers the survey flagged — Angelopoulos's non-monotonic CRC (arXiv:2602.20151) and Yu & Liu's
joint selective certificate (arXiv:2606.08517) — are cited as *generic machinery we instantiate*,
not competitors. This keeps the claim at "certified selective triage for ASR" and never drifts
to "conformal ASR" (which Ernez et al. own).

**Reproducibility is the submission's strongest card.** Every figure and table regenerates
from frozen result JSONs via `compute_numbers.py` + `figures/make_figures.py`; the tool's
golden-file tests reproduce the paper's numbers bit-for-bit. TASLP has no formal
"certification" stamp like TMLR's, but it values reproducibility highly and accepts code/data
availability statements and supplementary artifacts (Zenodo/HF DOI) that directly serve its
empirical bar — so this evidence carries over intact; only the OpenReview-specific
"Reproducibility Certification" request is dropped.

**Gaps that are honestly disclosed, not fatal (each strengthens the paper as candor):**
single primary certified backbone (Paraformer); Conformer-Aishell excluded as degraded (the
ModelScope checkpoint exposes tokens+text but no per-token posteriors → s1/s2 uncomputable →
`SKIPPED_DEGRADED`, never imputed); s3/s4/s5 structurally null on Paraformer (no N-best margin,
no full posteriors); main-run Holm family only m=2 (the expansion's m=12 is what carries the
multiplicity claim); speaker exchangeability strained by 20-test-speaker blocking; Paraformer
industrial-checkpoint contamination cannot be excluded (mitigated by the Whisper and noise arms,
whose confidence signal is independent of any Aishell-test memorization). **Verdict: enough.**

## 2. Is the FORMAT suitable?

**Yes — TASLP is a full-length Transactions with no restrictive page limit, so the honest
apparatus fits; a 4-page ICASSP condensation would not.** TASLP uses the double-column IEEEtran
class; Transactions papers typically run ~11–14 pages including references (overlength page
charges may apply beyond the target, but there is no hard cap that forces content out). The
paper's value is in the *honest apparatus*: the disclosed-deviation table (WeNet dropped,
HB→EB+Bonferroni pivot with the pilot power-failure trace, ESC-50-for-MUSAN substitution, THCHS
mirror-route substitution, roster-derived m falling 10→2→12), the macro-vs-micro CER caveat
table, the speaker-exchangeability candor paragraph, the vacuity interpretation, and the full
m=12 Holm table with permutation and Holm-adjusted p-values. None of that survives a 4-page cut
honestly — the design's own note says the Holm audit "does not fit 4 pages honestly." The
current 12-page single-column draft maps to roughly 11–13 IEEEtran double-column pages (see §4);
that is comfortably within TASLP norms, and any pressure is relieved by moving the four appendix
tables to a supplementary file, which TASLP accepts. ICASSP 2027 (deadline Sept 16, 2026; a
4-page C1+tool condensation) is a conference footnote option only, not a substitute for the
journal submission.

## 4. Packaging: IEEEtran conversion cost (bounded, ~1.5–2 days)

The draft is deliberately written content-first in the journal-agnostic `article` class (house
`reliability-commons` pattern), so the port is mechanical, not a rewrite:

- **Class swap:** `\documentclass[journal]{IEEEtran}`; replace `authblk`
  `\author`/`\affil` with IEEEtran's `\author{...}` block and `\IEEEauthorblockN/A`. Drop
  `geometry` (IEEEtran sets its own margins), `parskip`, and the `\setlength{\parskip}` line.
- **Bibliography:** switch `\bibliographystyle{plainnat}` → `IEEEtran` (numeric); the `natbib`
  `\citep/\citet` calls become `\cite`. `refs.bib` + `shared.bib` are reused unchanged. Budget
  ~1 hour to reconcile natbib→IEEEtran citation commands (a scripted sed pass covers most).
- **Figures:** the five PDFs are already vector and sized in `\linewidth` fractions; in double
  column they render at column width (`\includegraphics[width=\columnwidth]{...}`) or, for the
  two wide figures (F4 noise 2-panel, and optionally F2/F5), `figure*` spanning both columns.
  No regeneration needed — `make_figures.py` output is resolution-independent. Re-tune the
  `\linewidth` fractions to `\columnwidth`.
- **Tables:** `booktabs` is fine in IEEEtran; wide tables (T2 Holm m=12, T4 deviations) use
  `table*` to span both columns or move to supplementary. `siunitx` and `\small` carry over.
- **Math/macros:** `amsmath`/`amssymb` load under IEEEtran; `cleveref` works; keep the `\cer`,
  `\accset` macros. `microtype` stays.
- **Risk items:** IEEEtran + `cleveref` + `hyperref` ordering occasionally warns — load order
  is already correct in the draft (hyperref before cleveref). Double-column float placement of
  the five figures + four tables is the only fiddly part; the supplementary-file escape hatch
  removes that pressure.

Net: a scripted class/bib swap plus figure-width re-tuning and float placement, ~1.5–2 days
including a clean IEEEtran build and proofread. No content changes; the seven referee items and
all numbers are venue-agnostic and move verbatim.

## Decision points

### D1 — Optional Common Voice zh-CN transfer arm: **SKIP for initial submission.**

*Recommendation:* do not add it before the roster freezes; hold it as the top reviewer-response
experiment. *Justification:*

1. **The multi-* story is already delivered without it.** The expansion gives a second corpus
   (THCHS-30 cross-corpus), a second certified backbone lane (Whisper), three noise levels, and
   an m=12 realized Holm family — the "new modality, transplanted apparatus" claim (C3) is
   already demonstrated across architectures, noise, and corpora.
2. **It would most likely add a second *vacuous* certificate, not a rescue.** Common Voice
   zh-CN is spontaneous/accented; the only backbone we ran cross-corpus is Whisper, which is
   already vacuous on THCHS-30. Certifying Common Voice with Whisper would very likely be vacuous
   too; a non-vacuous cross-corpus certificate would need Paraformer applied cross-corpus (not
   yet run) and is not guaranteed to clear α=2% either. The honest vacuity finding already in
   hand does not need this experiment to publish.
3. **It costs a roster-freeze reopening + GPU box + prereg amendment** for marginal evidence,
   against a submission that is otherwise CPU-only and ready. TASLP's rigor/reproducibility bar
   is already cleared by the evidence in hand; the extra arm buys robustness, not admissibility.
4. **It is the ideal reviewer-response arm precisely because it reuses the THCHS apply/audit
   machinery verbatim** (CC0 license — the cleanest possible), ≤6 GPU-h on one 4090D. If a
   referee asks for a non-vacuous cross-corpus certificate, run Paraformer on Common Voice
   zh-CN then; the amendment and compute are pre-scoped.

*Consequence for the draft:* freeze the dataset roster now at the expansion roster; write the
cross-corpus story around THCHS-30 (audit transfers, certificate vacuous on the weak backbone),
and note Common Voice zh-CN as explicit future/response work.

### D2 — Certified-backbone framing: **lead with Paraformer C1, use Whisper as the vacuity/audit
contrast, disclose Conformer as degraded.** Do not attempt to revive Conformer or WeNet for the
certificate; the degraded-mode exclusion is the honest apparatus behaving as designed.

### D3 — Reproducibility packaging: **ship a code+data availability statement with a Zenodo/HF
DOI at submission.** The JSON-traced figure/number pipeline already satisfies it; TASLP has no
OpenReview-style certification stamp, so the artifact link in the availability statement is the
vehicle. This is the submission's strongest card.

### D4 — Format: **convert to IEEEtran (double-column) at submission**, per §4 (~1.5–2 days,
mechanical). Keep drafting and iterating in the current `article` class; port only once content
is frozen, and move the four appendix tables to a supplementary file if double-column float
placement gets tight.
